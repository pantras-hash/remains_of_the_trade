Methodology

What the indices are designed to capture

The paper separates third-country exposure into four channels that need not identify the same countries as most exposed. Each is derived from an Armington-CES model rather than from trade-basket overlap. The similarity indices are included for comparison: the paper shows they are a weak guide to incidence, since they are symmetric and ignore the size of the product-level shock, Chinese market penetration, substitution elasticities, and incidence weights.

01

Export competition

Chinese exports displaced from the U.S. are redirected to third markets, eroding other exporters' sales in shared product-destination cells. ECI predicts the resulting percentage decline in a country's exports.

02

Import competition

Those same redirected goods raise import penetration in a country's own market. ICI predicts the percentage decline in domestic sales of import-competing producers.

03

Consumer gains

Cheaper Chinese supply lowers the prices buyers pay. CGI predicts the percentage decline in import prices, not in the overall consumer price index.

04

Sourcing gains

Cheaper Chinese intermediate inputs cut production costs downstream. SGI predicts the percentage increase in a country's exports, tracing input-output links.
